# ADR candidate — multi-process admission revocation

**PROPOSED / OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED.** This is a security contract and design comparison, not an implementation, schema change, owner approval or change to D-04. Source: [PR #34](https://github.com/Burn2288/FLOW-BRIDGE-GEN/tree/904aa95e7cc6c42b93c8567bbb86c88832021449), [PR #36](https://github.com/Burn2288/FLOW-BRIDGE-GEN/tree/6fb9387f7b57300f7e8418cd8a250815e44a0cb4), [independent audit](https://github.com/Burn2288/FLOW-BRIDGE-GEN-AUDIT/pull/19#issuecomment-6959165306). Foundation contracts [C-001/003](https://github.com/Burn2288/FLOW-BRIDGE-GEN/blob/6697bfa5ce1a4f5c20c29cd628b2151db1fe306d/docs/contracts/IDENTITY_CONSENT.md), [C-019/020/021](https://github.com/Burn2288/FLOW-BRIDGE-GEN/blob/6697bfa5ce1a4f5c20c29cd628b2151db1fe306d/docs/contracts/ADMIN_CONTROL.md), [C-026](https://github.com/Burn2288/FLOW-BRIDGE-GEN/blob/6697bfa5ce1a4f5c20c29cd628b2151db1fe306d/docs/contracts/CONTRACT_REGISTRY.md) remain DESIGN_DRAFT. Owner-approved private-pilot intent admits admins and separate non-admin testers and rejects outsiders before registration; this proposal does not change that decision.

## Current behavior and limits

At #36, `admission.admitted` reads process-local `Config.admin_ids`/`pilot_user_ids`; Telegram checks a received update before identity creation, `Service.identity` checks again, and Mini App login and session use consult their local `cfg`. `Service.authorize` also checks existing user/consent. #36 actually observed stale B and Mini App continue to permit the tester while A was restarted; 6.338 s is the test sequence duration, not a global maximum. Changing an environment variable cannot update an already running process. The administrative session is separate, process-local; admission alone must never grant admin privileges.

## Proposed terms and authorization boundary

`AdmissionPolicy`: mode, permitted identities, independent admin roles, validity and policy revision. `AdmissionVersion`: monotonically ordered policy revision (no timestamp comparison as authority). `AdmissionDecision`: allow/deny plus action, authenticated subject, checked version and reason category. `PolicyUpdatedAt`: transaction commit timestamp or audit metadata, not a clock used for ordering. `SessionIssuedAt`: issuance evidence; a valid session is authentication, never permanent admission. `RequestAuthorizedAt`: the policy-check linearization point for the specific protected action, recorded by version/order rather than trusting wall clocks. `RevocationEffectiveAt`: **B:** successful commit of the serialized revocation; **A:** only after ingress is fenced and every previously serving instance is drained/stopped/fenced and the coordinator commits a verified rollout barrier. A policy-edit request or a single restarted process is not effectiveness.

Every callback or HTTP operation authenticates server-side and checks current admission at its protected action boundary. Admin: admission plus independently verified admin role/session and action-specific authorization; tester: only explicitly allowed pilot actions, no admin elevation; outsider: no identity/user/wallet creation via protected entry points. Public mode, consent, model/provider/payment flags and RBAC remain independent. Errors do not leak identity enumeration, raw initData, session tokens or policy secrets.

Distinguish login, read own data, quote, confirm/create generation, admin, already-authorized completion and retrieval of previously paid results. The existing owner decision forbids new commands/jobs/topups after revocation and preserves data; **which narrow authenticated support/retrieval path remains available and who can authorize it is an owner decision**, not a grant made by this ADR. Completion, settlement and authenticated payment reconciliation must preserve accounting and durable paid results; revocation is not deletion, automatic refund or cancellation. Administrative and service principals cannot be inferred from tester status.

## A — coordinated process rollout (not currently implemented)

Inventory all serving Telegram consumers, Mini App/API instances, background entry points and direct ingress; block/drain new protected requests at every ingress, fence or terminate all old holders (including partitions, restart and stale token routes), identify pre-barrier work, roll out the exact revision, verify each expected instance's version and health, and open only when a durable rollout barrier is complete. Unknown, unreachable or unregistered instances keep the gate closed; lease expiry alone is unsafe unless an independent data-path fence prevents that instance from executing. Rollback is another fenced, audited policy revision; it cannot resurrect an old instance or reverse settled financial work. Already-authorized operations must follow the separate completion policy; drain/fence must not drop provider reconciliation or authenticated financial events.

Guarantee is conditional on complete discovery, fail-closed routing, fencing and durable acknowledgements. Existing infrastructure does not prove any of those, and cannot assert an upper bound: a missing process or partition may keep service closed indefinitely. A finite SLO is a separate owner-approved availability/security tradeoff, not a guarantee from restart. Operational coordination and downtime grow with fleet size; no DB schema is intrinsically required, but a durable coordinator/fence or equivalent trusted mechanism must be designed and tested.

## B — centralized authoritative policy (not currently implemented)

Proposed PostgreSQL authority: single policy metadata row with monotonic version and audited revision, separate subject/role membership keyed by verified external identity and mode, and append-only policy change events. Atomic update uses a row lock or compare-and-swap on the expected version; identical idempotency key + same payload returns the prior result, conflicting payload rejects; concurrent updates cannot silently overwrite each other. Privileged change requires independently authenticated, authorized admin, explicit audit actor/correlation/reason, immutable before/after digest and protected audit trail. Initial bootstrap, break-glass recovery, multiple admins, D-04 env compatibility, rollout/backfill and downgrade policy need owner approval. No SQL is supplied or applied here.

For sensitive state changes, check identity/admission and policy version **inside the same DB transaction** as the durable quote/confirm/job intent using a compatible row lock shared with the revoke update. Serialize authorization against revocation: a revocation that commits first denies the subsequent protected mutation; if an authorization commits first, it is a pre-revocation operation and follows the approved completion/settlement policy. `quote` is not authority for `confirm`: confirm rechecks under the lock. The transaction must atomically preserve quote, wallet reserve/ledger and job intent, obey a documented global lock order with existing pricing/wallet locks, and commit before external provider calls. Do not hold a policy/financial lock across network calls; later external outcomes reconcile idempotently. In-progress HTTP responses and external sends cannot be retroactively undone; a read begun before revocation may have already disclosed data and requires an explicit policy.

Each Mini App request revalidates the authenticated subject's current admission even if an unexpired bearer session exists; Telegram callback and alternate endpoints go through the same server-side boundary. Session invalidation can supplement but cannot replace the policy check. No stale-positive authorization cache is allowed for protected actions unless an enforceable version fence supplies equivalent linearizability; TTL and notification-only invalidation alone cannot give immediate revocation. With DB unavailable or policy version unprovable, deny new protected user actions and fail the admin update closed; preserve durable pending work and process authenticated payment/provider callbacks using a separately specified service authorization and reconciliation path. Never rely on an emergency bypass hidden in a client.

Guarantees depend on exhaustive endpoint coverage, authenticated identities, consistent primary DB reads (not stale replicas), transaction isolation/lock ordering, audited admin control and a fail-closed implementation. PostgreSQL unavailability reduces availability. Read-only endpoints require their own defined serialization/response boundary; no claim that a check before streaming prevents an already-started response from finishing.

## Comparison and failure matrix

| Criterion | A: fenced rollout | B: centralized policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New-request revocation | After verified global fence/barrier; bounded only with enforceable assumptions | After authoritative committed version, for routes actually checked against primary |
| Stale instance | Must be stopped or independently fenced | Cannot use local allow as authority on guarded path |
| Database outage | Coordinator/fence must keep ingress closed if policy cannot be verified | Fail closed for protected actions; preserve service reconciliation |
| Availability / performance | Rollout pause and operational coordination | Per-request DB/lock cost; needs capacity and contention measurement |
| Telegram / Mini App | Both need complete ingress inventory and drain | Both need current server-side checks, including old callbacks and bearer sessions |
| Jobs / Wallet | Drain must preserve committed jobs and payment events | Commit-order protocol and lock ordering protect reserve/ledger; no network under lock |
| Migrations / scale | No mandatory table, but trusted durable coordinator may need infrastructure | Additive policy tables/migration and backfill; rollout/rollback and indexes needed; more natural multi-instance scaling |

| Scenario | A proposed acceptance | B proposed acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revoke before new request | Fenced old and new instances deny after barrier | Primary version check denies after revoke commit |
| Revoke during request | Drain/fence and approved completion policy; operation already authorized is classified | Lock/commit order classifies mutation; prior reads/sends cannot be undone |
| Between quote and confirm | Confirm passes current revision after rollout | Confirm rechecks in financial transaction; old quote is not authorization |
| Old callback / Mini App session | Gate on serving instance after barrier; stale instance fenced | Recheck current policy on every protected route/session use |
| Stale or unreachable instance | Barrier cannot complete until instance fenced | Guarded requests fail closed if authority unavailable; unguarded route is a blocker |
| DB unavailable | Close gate when policy/barrier not provable | Reject new protected mutations; service reconciliation follows its separate contract |
| Repeated/concurrent update | Durable idempotent versioned barrier, no lost update | Transactional CAS/idempotency and audit; conflict rejected |
| Crash mid-revoke | Durable fence and recoverable rollout state, do not reopen early | Atomic commit/rollback and recover from audit/version; no partial policy |

## Recommendation and owner decisions

Recommend **B**, only after owner decision, as the long-term target: a single server-authoritative source with transactional ordering offers a more reviewable guarantee for multiple independently restarting processes than an unimplemented all-ingress operational fence. A controlled A-style maintenance gate may be needed to migrate safely, but a hybrid is not declared proven. Performance and availability costs require measurement. Neither option is approved or implemented by this package.

Owner must decide: A/B and effective-time semantics; rights for already-paid-result retrieval/support; pre-authorized but unfinished work and paid external outcomes; operational availability/SLO during DB loss; initial admin/bootstrap and D-04; reviewer/writer assignments and migration/rollback. Proposed migration is additive and reversible only before new policy data becomes authoritative: establish metadata/membership/audit tables, dual-read comparison with no authority switch, backfill validated env lists, fail-closed cutover with a tested rollback fence. Once authoritative policy changes occur, reverting to old env lists could regrant revoked users; rollback needs explicit reconciled state and owner authorization, not simply dropping tables.

Future evidence: real multi-process PostgreSQL tests for barriers/transaction ordering and concurrent CAS, session/callback routes, allowlist/idempotency, availability failures, worker crash/restart, unchanged wallet/ledger/payment/media bytes, no duplicate provider POST and post-revocation authorized service reconciliation. Cross-review vankhu21 covers Telegram/UI, financial/Wallet, migrations and shared-file locks; no request is made now. Existing VIDEO-DELIVERY-AMBIGUITY and IN-19 remain open.
